## Theory Page

### Requirements for Fusion

- Ion's must be high enough temperature to be raised to ignition point.
- Taking Boltzmann Temperature Definition of Temperature $E=k_B T$ [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronvolt). We find 1 eV = 11604.45 K.
- For Deuterium as a fuel starting at ambient temperature and pressure, a temperature of $10^8$K would cause some fusion, however the pressure of the mass at this temperature is 1.5*$10^5$ Bar. A self sustaining reaction should theoretically occur at 3.5*$10^8$ K.
- We require vaccuum to keep the pressure of our plasma within controllable bounds and protect the walls of the chamber.
- We will use a high voltage feedthrough for a negatively charged electrode, with the outer chamber being neutrally charged electrode.
- Assuming no losses 30kV should be sufficient as it allows deuterium nuclei to reach 3.48*$10^8$ K. (30,000 V -> 30,000 keV ->3.48*$10^8$ K).
- However the cathode Grid absorbs a lot of the power. The Lawson Criterion for Fusion is $Fusion Power  = Efficiency * [Fusion - Conduction - Radiation]$
- In a functional reactor, deuterium atoms are ionized into Deuterons (deuterium ions with charge of 1+).
- Kinetic energy gained through field $\Delta T= \int E(x) dx$. In the special case of Electrostatic Accelerator of a sufficiently large potential difference $V_0$ a particle of charge q gains energy $qV_0$  

### Ionization of Fuel Atoms

- When electron's in Deuterium gain enough energy (ionization energy) from a static electric field they are liberated from their atom and can potentially liberate other electrons.
- Free electrons can be generated by cosmic rays colliding with gas molecules, these are called "seeding electrons".
- The grid in the fusor releases electrons under high voltage. This chain reaction is called a Townsend Avalanche. The final state of this is the Townsend breakdown when the **gas becomes a conductor** due to the clouds of free electrons conducting between cathode and anode.
- Formation of plasma, an inert electrostatic confinement plasma can have particle density of $10^{32}$ per $m^3$ and the temperature of the same plasma can reach $10^8$ K.
- In IEC Plasma concentrates center of the hollow cathode. Starts to glow in high ion concentration, with higher voltage and current a plasmoid forms.
- Plasmoid has bugle, jet and star mode.

#### Arcing

- At too high pressures or starting potentials electric discharges may occur before we reach glow discharge mode.
- Voltage to start a discharge or electric arc can be described by Paschen's law.

$$V=\frac{apd}{ln(pd)+b}$$

V=breakdown voltage in Volts, 
p = pressure in Bar, 
d = gap distance in meters (anode cathode seperation). 
a and b are constants depending on the gas.

- Some Arcing data for deuterium found [here.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/511/1/012027/pdf) However, this data uses a significantly different geometry and does not have a large range.
- Shows that in high voltage region of interest breakdown occurs at 26-30kV and ranges from 7 to 16 mbar cm.

### Choice of Energy

- This is thermonuclear fusion, or hot fusion, not to be confused with cold fusion. 
- Ideal gas velocity distribution $v = (2KT/m)^{1/2}$ where v is velocity of the particle, K is boltzmann's constant 1.4*$10^{-23} JK^{-1}$, m is the mass in kg. T is the temperature in K.
- At >10keV fusion reactants exist in plasma state. The plasma turns into a Poisor (plasma focus) when a mean free path for particles (ions) has become **comparable to the vacuum chamber radius**.

Mean free path $$l = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2}\pi d^2p}$$

l is the mean free path for particles
$k_B$ boltzmann's constant in Joules per Kelvin
T is temperature in kelvin
d is diameter of gas particles in meters
p is the pressure in pascals

i.e. kb = 1.4e-23, T=300K (ambient), P=1 Pascal, d=2.74e-10 (equivalent scattering diameter of $D_2$, l = 0.013m.

- Exceeding three radii the particle will begin recirculation, enlarging mean free path the particle will oscillate back and forth until full path played out or until reaching a cathode wire. In a Farnsworth-Hirsch Fusor an ion generally makes about five recirculation passes before it ends against the grid (however this can be improved).

Fusion rate with a constant particle density is defined by $$f = n_1n_2\sigma v$$
